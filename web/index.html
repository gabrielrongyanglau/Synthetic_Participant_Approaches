Compare with human data Optional
Check how closely your completed AI datasets reproduce observed human scores and relationships.
This step requires your own human reference dataset with the same psychological variables and both life-satisfaction outcomes. Demographics are optional. All eligible supplied human records form the reference; rows are not matched one-to-one with generated participants. This file is used temporarily for the comparison. It is not sent to OpenAI or saved by the application.
Fictional demonstration comparisons show only how the measures work and do not evaluate an AI approach. Each dataset is scored separately. Confidence intervals describe precision across scorable repetitions, conditional on this fixed human reference. One repetition cannot estimate a confidence interval. Primary relationship metrics exclude anxiety because its correlations can be undefined in small samples. Undefined values appear blank, never as zero.

Findings from the original study
Supporting evidence for choosing an approach, separate from your current simulations.
The original MIDUS study compared eight methods across 1,000 repetitions per method, each with 100 participants, against the full reference of 2,709 eligible human participants. These findings apply to that study’s model, prompts and variables.
Different inputs support different purposes.
M5 reproduced generated psychological-score distributions most closely. M6 reproduced relationships most closely among approaches that generated psychological scores. M7 had the closest relationships overall and retained actual psychological scores as inputs. M8 shows that realistic demographics alone did not produce accurate psychological relationships.
High pattern correlations can coexist with large errors in relationship strengths. These results do not establish accurate individual predictions, valid replacement of missing responses or intervention effects.
