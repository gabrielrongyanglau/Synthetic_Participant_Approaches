Overview

The proposal compared these approaches with real responses from the Midlife in the United States (MIDUS) study. You can try out the toolkit with that MIDUS study, or enter your own.

How this works The MIDUS demonstration skips steps 3 and 6

  1. Try out the MIDUS study from the proposal, or enter your own study
  2. Choose your research goal
  3. For your own study, define your questionnaire
  4. Answer up to three questions about what you have
  5. Use the recommended approach or an alternative
  6. For your own study, provide the inputs the approach needs
  7. Check inputs and generate 100 participants
  8. Download the dataset and codebook

Generation uses GPT-5.6 Luna with medium reasoning. AI calls use the research team’s server-side key and its limited budget, so each simulation creates one dataset of 100 participants.

Define your study and questionnaire

Give every measure its definition, source, items and scoring. Participant data come later, when the approach needs them.

Your study measures, optional demographics and papers

Define every item and its scoring. The toolkit checks structure and ranges, and you verify sources and wording. Up to 30 measures and 60 item and demographic fields. Custom questionnaires extend the evaluated MIDUS setup and need their own validation.

Psychological measures

Demographic categories (optional)

Add these if you know the demographic make-up you want to simulate, or have each participant’s demographic details. Approaches that use this information need them. Define up to eight, such as age group or employment arrangement, using numeric categories.

Mean and sum scores need every item observed. Reverse coding and composite scoring run in the toolkit after generation.

Research context and supporting papers Optional

A research question, pasted findings or PDF papers can guide a custom simulation. They are reference inputs, and the method instructions stay fixed. Literature-informed simulation is an extension beyond the MIDUS evaluation.

A research question or hypothesis serves as context only. The simulation can produce results that differ from it.

Limits for supporting sources

Up to three sources and 60,000 characters of text in total. Each PDF must be under 1.5 MB and 40 pages. Text extraction makes no AI calls. For scanned or protected files, paste the relevant text. The model reads only the text supplied here, so include the findings alongside any citation or DOI.

Only the reviewed text shown here is sent during AI generation. The PDF files are read in memory and discarded after extraction. Extraction copies text as written, so check claims, citations and tables yourself.

What did the study find?

    
Approaches differ in which goal they serve best: reproducing psychological scores (Goal 1) or reproducing relationships among variables (Goal 2). These are the original MIDUS results: eight approaches, 1,000 datasets of 100 participants each, compared with 2,709 human participants. They are separate from your own simulations.

  


  

    
    
Changes the relationship charts. Score distributions always use baseline predictors.

  


  


  

    How to read these charts
    
For error measures, lower is closer to human data. For ICC, concordance and pattern correlation, higher is closer to the target of 1. Each dot is the mean across scorable datasets. Whiskers show the 95% confidence interval, which can be narrower than the dot. Narrow intervals mean average performance is precisely estimated. Accuracy is shown by the position of the dot.

    
Intervals are conditional on the fixed human reference, and the adjusted pairwise comparisons below test whether methods differ. SPA8 keeps actual psychological scores, so its distribution results describe supplied scores. Hover over or focus a point for its values.

Goal 2 · Relationship strengths
How accurately are relationships with life satisfaction reproduced?

    
Correlation mean absolute error averages the gaps between simulated and human correlations across 37 predictors. A correlation of .80 against a human .20 is an error of .60. Lower is closer.

    

    

    

  


  

    
Goal 2 · Pattern versus strength
A similar pattern can hide inaccurate relationship strengths.

    
Pattern r asks whether predictors with stronger human correlations also have stronger simulated ones. ICC(A,1) and Lin’s CCC ask whether the actual values agree. Higher is closer to 1 for all three.

    

    

    

    
Pattern correlations are high for every approach, including those with large relationship errors. Read them alongside agreement and absolute error.

Errors and help
Every message names the problem and what to do. Many problems are handled automatically.

  

    
SituationWhat happens

    
Wrong file type, separators or decimal commasA message names the file and the exact fix before anything is sent.

    
Wrong number of rows, missing columns or values out of rangeThe message gives the row, the column and what is allowed.

    
Names, emails or phone numbers in a fileThe upload is stopped until they are removed.

    
Sensitive topics such as suicide or self-harmAn extra confirmation of ethics approval and de-identification is needed.

    
The AI service is busy or briefly unavailableThe request is retried automatically.

    
A batch fails, expires, stalls or returns repetitive or unusable dataThat batch is requested once more automatically.

    
The research budget is used up or the service key is rejectedGeneration stops and you are asked to contact the research team.

    
The connection dropsProgress checks pause and resume when you are back online.
