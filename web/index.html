How to prepare a participant-data CSV
  1. Download the matching blank template and the filled fictional example for your selected approach.
  2. Keep the header row unchanged. Enter one participant per row. Use numeric codes from the variable guide, with no names, email addresses or other direct identifiers.
  3. For pilot approaches, supply at least 50 participant records. The tool draws 50 without replacement. In a custom study, include every raw questionnaire item and demographic variable. In MIDUS mode, include the listed scores, demographics and both life-satisfaction outcomes.
  4. For demographic profiles, supply at least 100 rows with the required columns. For profiles with psychology, include observed predictor scores as well. Outcomes are generated and need not be uploaded.
  5. Save as UTF-8 comma-separated CSV. Use blank cells only where missing values are allowed. Custom pilot items and demographics must be complete.
  6. Upload the file and choose Check inputs and review simulation. Errors will identify what needs correcting before any paid request.

Download templates after finishing your measure definitions. If you change the measures, download fresh templates. A fictional example demonstrates the format and is not human evidence.

Understanding the measures




What did the study find?


Different approaches perform best on different aspects of fidelity: how closely generated data reproduce human scores and relationships.


These are the original study’s findings.
The Midlife in the United States (MIDUS) study compared eight approaches across 1,000 repetitions per method, each with 100 participants, against the full reference of 2,709 eligible human participants. They are separate from your current simulations. Charts use the repetitions that could be scored.


This changes the relationship charts and findings below. Psychological-score distributions always refer to baseline predictors.




How to read these charts and confidence intervals
For error measures, lower values mean closer reproduction of human data. For ICC, concordance and pattern correlation, higher values are closer to the target of 1. In the first three charts, each dot is the mean across scorable repetitions; whiskers show its 95% confidence interval. The variable-level scatterplot shows average correlations without confidence intervals. Intervals can be narrower than the dots at this scale. Small whiskers reflect precise estimates of average performance and do not guarantee that the simulated data are accurate.
The intervals are conditional on the fixed human reference. Confidence-interval overlap alone does not establish whether methods differ. The detailed pairwise comparisons below provide the adjusted tests. SPA8 retains actual psychological scores, so its distribution results reflect supplied scores.

02 · RELATIONSHIP STRENGTHS
How accurately are relationships with life satisfaction reproduced?
Correlation mean absolute error averages the gaps between simulated and human correlations across 37 psychological predictors. A correlation of .80 versus .20 gives an error of .60. Lower is closer.


03 · PATTERN VERSUS AGREEMENT
A similar pattern can hide inaccurate relationship strengths
Pearson pattern r indicates whether predictors with higher human correlations also tend to have higher simulated correlations. The absolute-agreement intraclass correlation coefficient, ICC(A,1), and Lin’s concordance correlation coefficient (CCC) assess agreement in the actual coefficient values. Higher is closer to the target of 1 for all three measures.
Pattern correlations are high across methods, including those with large relationship errors. Check numerical agreement and absolute error alongside pattern correlation.
