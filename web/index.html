A research question, pasted findings or PDF papers can guide a custom simulation. They are reference inputs, not a prompt editor; the method instructions stay fixed. Literature-informed simulations were not part of the MIDUS evaluation.

Limits for supporting sources

Up to three sources and 60,000 characters of text in total. Each PDF must be under 1.5 MB and 40 pages. Text extraction makes no AI calls. Scanned or protected files may need the relevant text pasted instead. A citation or DOI alone does not let the model read a paper.

Only the reviewed text shown here is sent during AI generation. PDFs themselves are not sent to OpenAI or saved. Extraction does not verify claims, citations or table accuracy.

Inputs

How to prepare your CSV

Every upload uses the same layout: one row per participant and one column per variable. Download the template for your approach, replace the example values with your own, and upload it.

  • SPA4: a demographic sample of exactly 50 participants. The toolkit matches the proportions in this sample when it builds 100 demographic profiles.
  • SPA5 and SPA6: a pilot sample of exactly 50 participants with every variable, including both outcomes. All 50 are used.
  • SPA7: 100 participant profiles with demographics. Each row becomes one synthetic participant.
  • SPA8: 100 participant profiles with demographics and psychological scores. Each row becomes one synthetic participant, and only outcomes are generated.

In the MIDUS study each psychological column is a construct score. In your own study, each construct can be supplied either as its individual item columns or as one construct-score column. Keep the header row unchanged, use the numeric codes in the variable guide, remove names and other direct identifiers, and save as UTF-8 CSV. Choose Check inputs to see any problems before a paid request is made.

Compare with human data

Use observed human data with the same measures and outcomes. Custom studies can supply composite scores or the raw items needed to calculate them; demographics are optional. The file is used only for this comparison and is not sent to OpenAI or saved.

      

        
        
      

      

        How the comparison works
        
All eligible human records form the reference; rows are not matched one-to-one with generated participants. Each dataset is scored separately. One dataset cannot give a confidence interval.

        
The original MIDUS analysis excludes anxiety; custom analyses use all defined predictors. Pattern correlation needs at least three varying paired coefficients. Undefined values appear blank, never as zero.

        
An independent human benchmark is a stronger check when it was not used to guide generation. The original MIDUS results used the full eligible reference, including people who could supply input records, so the two answer different questions.

What did the study find?

    
Approaches differ in which aspect of fidelity they reproduce best. These are the original MIDUS results: eight approaches, 1,000 datasets of 100 participants each, compared with 2,709 human participants. They are separate from your own simulations.

  


  

    
    
Changes the relationship charts. Score distributions always use baseline predictors.

  


  


  

    How to read these charts
    
For error measures, lower is closer to human data. For ICC, concordance and pattern correlation, higher is closer to the target of 1. Each dot is the mean across scorable datasets; whiskers show the 95% confidence interval, which can be narrower than the dot. Narrow intervals mean average performance is precisely estimated, not that the data are accurate.

    
Intervals are conditional on the fixed human reference, and overlap alone does not show whether methods differ; see the adjusted pairwise comparisons below. SPA8 keeps actual psychological scores, so its distribution results describe supplied scores. Hover over or focus a point for its values.

Relationship strengths
How accurately are relationships with life satisfaction reproduced?

    
Correlation mean absolute error averages the gaps between simulated and human correlations across 37 predictors. A correlation of .80 against a human .20 is an error of .60. Lower is closer.

    

    

    

  


  

    
Pattern versus agreement
A similar pattern can hide inaccurate relationship strengths.

    
Pattern r asks whether predictors with stronger human correlations also have stronger simulated ones. ICC(A,1) and Lin’s CCC ask whether the actual values agree. Higher is closer to 1 for all three.

    

    

    

    
Pattern correlations are high for every approach, including those with large relationship errors. Read them alongside agreement and absolute error.
